Katheryn Wright

I make things. I work with students to make things. I love building infrastructure, from mobile making kits to theoretical frameworks that other people can actually use. The thread running through all of it is the place where theory becomes something you can hold, walk through, play with, or render.

I am drawn to big, complex projects but also to the small and intimate, the project or story that offers meaning in the middle of uncertainty.

My research sits at the intersection of digital humanities, AI ethics, place-based pedagogy, and critical posthumanism. My teaching and making practice spans portable printmaking kits, file folder games, mobile kitchens, digital textbooks, alternate reality games, and machine learning classifiers.

My book-in-progress, Speculative Education: Making Knowledge from the Ruins, asks what it looks like to make knowledge from what remains when the structures we counted on no longer hold. It is inspired by my mother, a teacher. It is about grief as much as it is about teaching.

I am a Full Professor of Digital Humanities and Roger H. Perry Endowed Chair for Innovation at Champlain College. I live in Colchester, Vermont with my husband and son.
